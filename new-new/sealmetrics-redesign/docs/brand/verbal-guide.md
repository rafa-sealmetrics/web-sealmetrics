# SealMetrics Verbal Brand Guide

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Working draft for founder review  
**Applies to:** Website, landing pages, campaigns, sales materials, product marketing, social content, newsletters, case studies and product copy  
**Languages:** English and Spanish  

---

## 1. Purpose of this guide

This document is the source of truth for how SealMetrics communicates.

It exists to make every piece of content feel as if it comes from the same company, with the same point of view and the same standard of evidence, regardless of who or what creates it.

The guide must help a writer, editor or AI system answer five questions:

1. Is this unmistakably SealMetrics?
2. Is the message relevant to the intended audience?
3. Is the argument clear enough to act on?
4. Are the claims precise and supportable?
5. Does the content earn trust rather than merely sound confident?

This is not a collection of preferred phrases. It is a decision system. The goal is not to make every page sound identical; the goal is to make every page express the same brand coherently.

## 2. Strategic foundation

### 2.1 What SealMetrics is

SealMetrics is consentless analytics for ecommerce, performance teams and agencies that need to understand traffic, attribution and revenue without making measurement depend on cookie consent or personal visitor profiles.

### 2.2 Category

**Primary category descriptor:** Consentless analytics  
**Broader category context:** Privacy-first analytics  
**Category ambition:** Become the standard for online measurement that does not depend on cookie consent.

Use **consentless analytics** when explaining what the product is. Use **privacy-first analytics** when placing SealMetrics within a broader market conversation. Do not use the two terms as if they were interchangeable.

### 2.3 The big idea

> **The analytics of reality.**

This is the brand territory, not a complete product description. When used in acquisition or sales content, it should normally appear alongside a concrete explanation of what SealMetrics does.

### 2.4 The problem we own

The problem is not a lack of dashboards. It is **unearned confidence in incomplete measurement**.

Consent rejection, blockers, browser restrictions and fragmented systems can remove or distort evidence before it reaches a report. Teams then make real budget decisions using a partial and potentially skewed picture.

### 2.5 The customer consequence

Incomplete measurement can:

- Make productive channels appear inefficient.
- Move sales into “Direct” or “Unknown”.
- Create conflicting numbers across marketing, agencies and finance.
- Turn budget allocation into an argument rather than a decision.
- Make AI analysis sound confident while reasoning from incomplete inputs.

### 2.6 The belief we defend

> More data is not the goal. Better decisions are. That requires less personal information, more useful evidence and a way to verify the result against the systems the business already trusts.

### 2.7 The enemy

The enemy is **not GA4 as a brand**. The enemy is the assumption that a polished dashboard must represent the whole business.

GA4, consent mode, modeled data and traditional tracking may be used as examples of the old measurement paradigm. They must not become the center of SealMetrics' identity.

**Rule:** Use GA4 as the wedge, not the identity.

### 2.8 The promise

SealMetrics gives teams a neutral measurement layer for making more defensible revenue and budget decisions from aggregate data that is not shaped by cookie consent.

### 2.9 Reasons to believe

Use only reasons that are technically verified and valid for the specific product configuration being described.

- Aggregate measurement without cookies, persistent visitor identifiers or fingerprinting.
- Measurement that does not depend on acceptance of an analytics banner.
- Revenue and conversion reporting that can be reconciled with backend systems.
- Side-by-side deployment with the customer's existing analytics stack.
- First-party implementation designed for fast time to value.
- European infrastructure and privacy-oriented data minimization.
- Named customer evidence and documented comparisons.

## 3. Positioning

### 3.1 Internal positioning statement

For ecommerce leaders, performance teams and agencies that cannot confidently connect marketing activity to business revenue, SealMetrics is a consentless analytics platform that measures aggregate traffic and outcomes without cookies or personal visitor profiles. Unlike consent-dependent analytics, SealMetrics lets teams compare measurement against backend truth and make budget decisions from evidence they can inspect.

This statement is an internal alignment tool. It should not be pasted into marketing copy unchanged.

### 3.2 External one-line explanation

> SealMetrics measures aggregate traffic and revenue without cookies or visitor profiles, so teams can make budget decisions from data that is not shaped by cookie consent.

### 3.3 Short descriptor

> Consentless analytics for defensible revenue decisions.

### 3.4 Message hierarchy

Content should move through this hierarchy in order. Do not lead with a feature when a business consequence can lead instead.

1. **Outcome:** Make a better or more defensible decision.
2. **Problem:** Important evidence is missing or distorted.
3. **Mechanism:** Aggregate, consentless measurement reconnects observable activity and outcomes.
4. **Proof:** Compare the result with the backend or a named customer case.
5. **Feature:** Pixel, reports, attribution, LENS, API, MCP, BigQuery or integrations.
6. **Action:** Run a fair test, inspect the evidence or start a side-by-side deployment.

### 3.5 Core narrative

The default SealMetrics argument follows five moves:

1. **Expose the uncomfortable gap.** The dashboard is confident; the inputs are incomplete.
2. **Show the business cost.** The gap changes channel rankings and budget decisions.
3. **Introduce the belief.** Better measurement should collect less personal data and preserve more useful evidence.
4. **Explain the mechanism.** SealMetrics measures aggregate events without creating visitor profiles or depending on analytics consent.
5. **Offer verification.** Run SealMetrics beside the existing stack and compare both with the backend.

## 4. Audience priorities

### 4.1 Primary audience: ecommerce and performance leaders

**What they need:** To know which channels, campaigns, products and funnel steps are producing revenue.  
**What they fear:** Cutting a winner, scaling a false positive or defending a number that does not reconcile.  
**What we promise:** A more defensible connection between observed acquisition activity and backend outcomes.  
**Best proof:** Store revenue, campaign comparison, SKU-level evidence and decisions made during live trading periods.  
**Tone:** Commercial, direct, specific and decision-oriented.

### 4.2 Primary distribution audience: agencies

**What they need:** A neutral measurement layer that clients will trust and that can be implemented consistently across accounts.  
**What they fear:** Spending QBRs defending attribution, losing client trust and absorbing fragmented measurement work.  
**What we promise:** Less time reconciling conflicting reports and more authority to recommend the next move.  
**Best proof:** Fast onboarding, backend reconciliation, repeatable deployment and partner economics.  
**Tone:** Advisory, commercially pragmatic and collaborative. Never imply that the agency caused the measurement problem.

### 4.3 Decision audience: CMOs and growth leaders

**What they need:** A marketing number finance can interrogate and the board can understand.  
**What they fear:** Budget decisions becoming political because every team has a different total.  
**What we promise:** A neutral record that turns measurement debate into a decision.  
**Best proof:** Named business outcomes, budget reallocations and reconciliation with revenue systems.  
**Tone:** Strategic, concise and outcome-led.

### 4.4 Influence audiences: data, technology, security, privacy and finance

**What they need:** A precise explanation of architecture, data flows, retention, security, legal roles and limitations.  
**What they fear:** Hidden identifiers, undefined claims, opaque subprocessors and avoidable risk.  
**What we promise:** Inspectable architecture, data minimization and documented controls.  
**Best proof:** Technical documentation, data-flow diagrams, contracts, policies and externally reviewable evidence.  
**Tone:** Sober, exact and respectful. No sarcasm, swagger or legal shortcuts.

### 4.5 Sector pages

Healthcare, education, media, SaaS, hotels and other verticals can provide relevance and SEO coverage. They must not make the core market appear undefined.

Each sector page must connect the same strategic foundation to a specific operational problem. Do not invent a new positioning for every vertical.

## 5. Voice

Voice is the stable personality of SealMetrics. It does not change by channel.

### 5.1 Direct

We reach the point quickly. We prefer specific consequences to abstract benefits.

**We sound like:** “The missing traffic changes which channel looks efficient.”  
**We do not sound like:** “Unlock enhanced visibility across your omnichannel customer journey.”

### 5.2 Evidence-led

We make arguments that can be inspected. Confidence comes from method and proof, not volume.

**We sound like:** “Run both tools for 14 days and compare them with recorded store revenue.”  
**We do not sound like:** “Trust the world's most accurate analytics platform.”

### 5.3 Constructively rebellious

We challenge accepted practices when they produce bad decisions. We attack the flawed assumption, not the intelligence or integrity of the reader.

**We sound like:** “A polished report does not repair missing inputs.”  
**We do not sound like:** “Anyone still using GA4 has no idea what they are doing.”

### 5.4 Commercially useful

Every argument should help the reader understand a risk, make a decision or take a next step.

**We sound like:** “Protect the channel that generated the sale before moving next week's budget.”  
**We do not sound like:** “The future of analytics has arrived.”

### 5.5 Composed

We are willing to be provocative, but never needy, frantic or theatrical. We do not use aggression as a substitute for authority.

**We sound like:** “If the backend does not confirm the difference, keep your current setup.”  
**We do not sound like:** “GA4 is dead. Switch now before it destroys your ROAS.”

## 6. Tone

Voice stays constant. Tone changes with context. The same person can challenge a market assumption on the homepage and answer a security review with restraint.

### 6.1 Default editorial balance

As a practical target:

- **70% clarity:** Explain the problem, mechanism and consequence.
- **20% tension:** Challenge an assumption or expose a contradiction.
- **10% wit:** Use a memorable turn of phrase when it adds meaning.

This is not a mathematical quota. It is a guardrail against making every line a punchline.

### 6.2 Tone by context

| Context | Provocation | Technical depth | Emotional energy | Rule |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Homepage | High | Medium | High | Open with tension; close with proof. |
| Paid campaign | High | Low-Medium | High | One idea, one contradiction, one action. |
| Product page | Medium | High | Medium | Mechanism and use case must dominate. |
| Comparison page | Medium-High | High | Medium | Compare architectures and evidence, not caricatures. |
| Case study | Low | Medium-High | Medium | Let the customer, method and numbers carry the story. |
| Founder LinkedIn | High | Variable | High | Personal observation is welcome; unsupported certainty is not. |
| Newsletter | Medium-High | Medium-High | Medium-High | Teach through a real case, comparison or field note. |
| Product interface | Low | Medium | Low | Be useful, calm and unambiguous. |
| Security, privacy and legal | Very low | Very high | Low | Precision outranks personality. |

### 6.3 The provocation rule

Use provocation to earn attention, then replace it with an argument.

- One strong contradiction per content unit is usually enough.
- Do not stack “truth”, “fiction”, “theatre”, “wrong”, “broken” and “reality” in the same section.
- Never leave a provocative claim unsupported when support could reasonably be expected.
- Never humiliate the reader for using the incumbent product.

## 7. Writing principles

### 7.1 Lead with the decision

Prefer the business move over the product capability.

**Prefer:** “Protect the campaigns that are actually producing recorded revenue.”  
**Avoid:** “Access advanced channel reporting.”

### 7.2 Make the mechanism visible

Do not imply magic. Explain why the result differs.

**Prefer:** “Measurement does not stop when a visitor rejects analytics cookies.”  
**Avoid:** “Recover invisible customers with proprietary technology.”

### 7.3 Separate observation from interpretation

An event can be observed. Revenue can be reconciled. Attribution is produced under a declared model. A recommendation is an interpretation.

Do not collapse these stages into a claim of causal certainty.

### 7.4 Write in contrasts, but not formulas

Contrast is a signature device:

- Dashboard vs backend.
- Consent-shaped subset vs aggregate measurement.
- Report vs decision.
- Personal tracking vs business measurement.
- More data vs better evidence.

Do not force every headline into “Not X. Y.” or “No X. Just Y.” Vary rhythm and construction.

### 7.5 Be concrete

Prefer:

- The named channel.
- The recorded sale.
- The backend total.
- The time of the decision.
- The attribution model.
- The precise implementation condition.

Avoid vague references to transformation, visibility, intelligence or growth when a concrete consequence is available.

### 7.6 Use short sentences deliberately

Short sentences create authority when surrounded by enough explanation. A page made entirely of fragments feels performative.

Use a mix of:

- Short lines for emphasis.
- Medium sentences for explanation.
- Occasional longer sentences for nuance and conditions.

### 7.7 Respect the reader

Assume the reader is intelligent and appropriately skeptical. Answer the obvious objection before it becomes a sales objection.

Good SealMetrics copy is comfortable saying:

- “This does not create revenue that did not happen.”
- “Attribution is not the same as causality.”
- “The result depends on the events and commerce data implemented.”
- “If the backend does not confirm a meaningful difference, keep your current setup.”

### 7.8 End with a verifiable next step

The strongest SealMetrics CTA is not “believe us”. It is “test this”.

## 9. Definitions that protect message consistency

### 9.1 Complete

“Complete” does not mean omniscient, causally perfect or immune to implementation failure.

When used by SealMetrics, it must refer to a defined scope, such as aggregate measurement that is not reduced by analytics-cookie rejection. The nearby copy or supporting documentation must make that scope understandable.

**Preferred:** “Complete observed data, without consent loss.”  
**Avoid:** “A complete record of everything every customer did.”

### 9.2 Observed

“Observed” means that an eligible event reached and was processed by the measurement system under the stated implementation. It does not mean that every event that happened in the world was captured.

### 9.3 Verified revenue

Use “verified revenue” only when the revenue total has been reconciled with an identified backend or system of record under a documented method.

If it has not been reconciled, use “recorded revenue” or “observed conversion revenue”, depending on the implementation.

### 9.4 Attribution

Attribution assigns credit under a declared model. It does not prove causality.

**Preferred:** “Connect observed sales to their recorded acquisition source under a consistent attribution model.”  
**Avoid:** “Prove which channel caused every sale.”

### 9.5 Consentless

“Consentless” describes a measurement architecture that does not depend on analytics consent for the defined use case. It is not, by itself, a universal legal conclusion.

The legal position may depend on jurisdiction, purpose, configuration, collected signals, contracts, transparency, retention and the customer's wider processing activities.

### 9.6 Neutral

“Neutral” means that SealMetrics is positioned as an independent measurement layer rather than a channel selling the media being measured.

Do not use “neutral” to imply mathematical or methodological absence of assumptions. Attribution rules, filters and definitions must still be declared.

## 10. Claims and evidence standard

SealMetrics sells trust in measurement. Our marketing claims must therefore meet a higher standard than ordinary SaaS copy.

### 10.1 Absolute claims

Examples:

- “100% of traffic.”
- “Every sale.”
- “Zero personal data.”
- “Legally measure without consent.”
- “No security risk.”

Before publication, an absolute claim requires:

1. Exact definition of the numerator and denominator.
2. Technical owner.
3. Evidence source.
4. Date or version to which the evidence applies.
5. Product configuration and exclusions.
6. Jurisdictions in scope, where relevant.
7. Legal review for legal or compliance conclusions.
8. A nearby qualification or accessible methodology when needed.

If any requirement is missing, rewrite the claim or block publication.

### 10.2 Comparative claims

Examples:

- “GA4 sees 42%.”
- “40–60% of EU visits are missing.”
- “Enterprise data without the $150K invoice.”
- “More accurate than another platform.”

A comparative claim must identify:

- The comparison group or product.
- The source and date.
- Whether the result is typical, observed in one customer or illustrative.
- Material differences in configuration.
- The measurement method.

Never present one customer's result as a universal market baseline.

### 10.3 Customer claims

A customer result should include, where possible:

- Named customer.
- Period measured.
- Baseline.
- SealMetrics configuration.
- Comparison method.
- Result.
- Business decision or consequence.
- Material caveat.
- Approval to publish.

### 10.4 Illustrative examples

Illustrative numbers are useful for explaining a mechanism. Label them next to the example, not in a distant footer.

**Required label:** “Illustrative example — not a live account.”  
Do not place invented numbers beside customer logos in a way that implies endorsement or provenance.

### 10.5 Legal and compliance claims

Marketing may explain architecture and data minimization. It must not substitute a slogan for a legal assessment.

**Prefer:**

- “Built for aggregate measurement without cookies or visitor profiles.”
- “Designed to minimize personal-data collection.”
- “Measurement does not depend on acceptance of analytics cookies.”
- “See the applicable architecture, purpose and safeguards in our trust documentation.”

**Avoid without explicit approval and scope:**

- “Measure 100%, legally.”
- “Nothing personal to consent to.”
- “Automatically GDPR compliant.”
- “Zero legal risk.”
- “Works without consent in every country.”

## 12. Signature content devices

These are repeatable editorial formats, not phrases that must appear everywhere.

### 12.1 Uncomfortable Truth

Use to challenge a widely accepted assumption.

**Structure:**

1. Accepted belief.
2. Contradicting observation.
3. Business consequence.
4. Evidence or method.
5. Action.

**Example opening:** “Your ROAS may be calculated correctly and still lead to the wrong budget decision.”

### 12.2 Reality Check

Use to compare a reported number with a backend, second measurement layer or documented baseline.

**Structure:**

1. What the dashboard reports.
2. What the system of record reports.
3. Why the gap exists.
4. Which decision changes.

### 12.3 The Monday Number

Use to dramatize organizational alignment.

**Structure:**

1. The number marketing brings.
2. The objection finance or the agency raises.
3. The reconciliation method.
4. The shared decision.

### 12.4 The Fair Test

Use to convert skepticism without demanding trust.

**Structure:**

1. Keep the current stack.
2. Install SealMetrics beside it.
3. Define the comparison period and backend baseline.
4. Compare coverage, revenue and channel allocation.
5. Keep the setup that produces the most useful, supportable result.

### 12.5 Proof, Not Promises

Use for customer evidence.

**Structure:**

1. Named customer and situation.
2. Measurement problem.
3. Method.
4. Result.
5. Decision or operational change.
6. Caveat or scope.

## 13. Channel guidance

### 13.1 Homepage

The homepage should communicate one central argument:

> Consent-shaped measurement can change budget decisions. SealMetrics provides a separate aggregate measurement layer that the customer can verify against backend outcomes.

The homepage may be provocative, but each major claim should lead toward mechanism or proof. Avoid introducing every product capability before the core argument is understood.

### 13.2 Product pages

Start with the decision enabled, then show the workflow and feature. Each product capability must answer:

- What decision changes?
- What evidence becomes available?
- How is the result produced?
- How can the user verify it?

### 13.3 Comparison pages

Compare the relevant architecture, measurement scope, assumptions, implementation and cost. Do not imply that the competing product has no valid use case.

The conclusion should be a test, not a declaration of victory.

### 13.4 Case studies

Write like a documented business analysis, not a testimonial montage. The customer should be the protagonist. SealMetrics is the mechanism that makes the decision possible.

### 13.5 Founder content

Rafael's voice can be more personal, raw and opinionated than the corporate brand. It should still follow the same evidence standard.

Recommended structure:

1. First-hand observation.
2. Uncomfortable conclusion.
3. Specific example or evidence.
4. Implication for the reader.
5. Practical action.

Founder content may say “I believe”. Corporate content should distinguish belief from substantiated fact.

### 13.6 Newsletter

Recommended recurring rhythm:

- **Technical case:** One measurement problem, method and decision.
- **Direct comparison:** One architectural difference and its consequence.
- **Founder field note:** What happened, what changed Rafael's mind and what the reader can apply.

### 13.7 Product interface

Interface copy prioritizes comprehension and recovery over brand attitude.

- Use familiar terminology.
- Explain what happened.
- Explain what the user can do next.
- Do not joke in error, billing, security or data-loss states.

### 13.8 Security, privacy and legal content

Use plain language, defined terms and inspectable facts. Avoid competitive attacks, metaphors that imply certification and broad legal conclusions.

## 15. Editorial validation rubric

Every material piece of SealMetrics content should be checked against this rubric.

Score each dimension from 0 to 2:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic alignment | Contradicts positioning | Partially aligned | Reinforces the core argument |
| Audience relevance | Generic | Audience named but shallow | Specific problem, consequence and context |
| Clarity | Confusing or jargon-heavy | Understandable with effort | Immediately clear |
| Evidence | Unsupported | Some support or qualification | Claim is scoped and verifiable |
| Distinctiveness | Generic SaaS | Some brand character | Recognizably SealMetrics |
| Tone control | Flat or over-aggressive | Mostly appropriate | Right volume for the context |
| Actionability | No meaningful next step | Generic CTA | Concrete, low-friction next step |

**Passing score:** 12/14 or higher, with no hard failure.

### Hard failures

Content must be blocked or rewritten if it contains any of the following:

- An unsupported absolute, legal or comparative claim.
- A customer number without provenance or approval.
- An illustrative number presented as a real result.
- A statement that treats attribution as proven causality.
- A contradiction of the primary positioning.
- A competitor attack that replaces the customer problem.
- A feature claim with no understandable customer consequence.
- A tone inappropriate for security, privacy, legal, billing or error contexts.
- A CTA that promises an experience the next step does not provide.

## 16. Validation and rewrite protocol

This protocol is designed to become the operating logic of a future SealMetrics content skill.

### Step 1: Establish context

Identify:

- Audience.
- Channel.
- Funnel stage.
- Desired action.
- Language.
- Required facts and available evidence.

If these are materially unclear, request clarification before writing.

### Step 2: Identify the one message

Write one sentence describing what the reader should understand or do after consuming the content. Remove secondary ideas that compete with it.

### Step 3: Check strategic alignment

Confirm that the message connects to at least one of:

- Better revenue or budget decisions.
- Missing or distorted measurement evidence.
- Aggregate measurement without visitor profiling.
- Verification against a backend or existing system.
- Organizational alignment around a neutral number.

### Step 4: Classify every claim

Mark each claim as:

- Product fact.
- Customer result.
- Comparative claim.
- Legal or compliance claim.
- Interpretation.
- Opinion.
- Illustrative example.

Apply the relevant evidence standard. Do not silently turn interpretation into fact.

### Step 5: Build the argument

When appropriate, order the copy as:

1. Consequence.
2. Problem.
3. Mechanism.
4. Proof.
5. Action.

### Step 6: Apply voice and tone

Check that the copy is direct, evidence-led, constructively rebellious, useful and composed. Adjust the level of provocation to the channel.

### Step 7: Remove verbal inflation

Remove or reduce:

- Repeated brand words.
- Unnecessary absolutes.
- Generic SaaS language.
- Multiple punchlines in the same section.
- Claims of certainty that exceed the evidence.
- Features that do not change the argument.

### Step 8: Produce a verdict

The future validator should return one of three outcomes:

- **PASS:** Publishable without material changes.
- **REWRITE:** Strategically sound but requires correction; provide the improved version.
- **BLOCKED:** Cannot be responsibly rewritten without missing evidence, legal approval or essential context.

### Step 9: Explain material changes

When rewriting, report only changes that affect:

- Positioning.
- Meaning.
- Evidence or qualification.
- Tone.
- Audience relevance.
- CTA.

Do not burden the user with a log of minor stylistic edits.

## 18. Governance

### 18.1 Source of truth

This document is the canonical verbal guide. Channel playbooks and prompts may extend it but must not contradict it.

### 18.2 Claim registry

Before this guide becomes a production skill, SealMetrics should maintain a claim registry containing:

- Approved claim.
- Approved wording variants.
- Definition and scope.
- Evidence link.
- Evidence owner.
- Date reviewed.
- Required qualification.
- Legal approval status.
- Channels where it may be used.

### 18.3 Change control

Changes to positioning, category, primary audience, definitions or restricted claims require founder approval. Routine examples and channel guidance can evolve without changing the strategic foundation.

### 18.4 Review cadence

Review this guide when:

- The primary ICP changes.
- Product architecture changes.
- A major capability is launched.
- Legal guidance materially changes.
- New customer evidence changes the core argument.
- Repeated content reviews reveal a rule that is unclear or counterproductive.

## 19. Decisions to resolve before version 1.0

The following decisions should be confirmed before converting this guide into a production skill:

1. **Primary category:** Confirm “consentless analytics” as the main descriptor and “privacy-first analytics” as the broader context.
2. **Primary commercial focus:** Confirm ecommerce/performance as the main buyer and agencies as the primary distribution audience.
3. **Definition of complete:** Approve the precise technical scope in which “complete data” may be used.
4. **Definition of verified revenue:** Approve the reconciliation method required before using this term.
5. **Approved legal language:** Define where and under which configurations “without consent”, “lawful” and related claims may appear.
6. **Approved quantitative claims:** Validate 100%, 40–60%, 846 B, installation times, customer results, pricing comparisons and infrastructure statements.
7. **Brand/product relationship:** Decide when Rafael speaks as founder and when SealMetrics speaks institutionally.
8. **Language adaptation:** Decide whether Spanish content should preserve the English level of provocation or use a more locally natural register.

Once these decisions are closed and the claim registry exists, the guide can be promoted to version 1.0 and converted into a content validation and rewrite skill.
